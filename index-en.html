Privacy Policy — FamilyStars | نجوم الأسرة

Developer: NOOR ALDIN ALNASSAR

Support, privacy enquiries and deletion requests: familystars.support@gmail.com.

Document version: 1.0.0-rc.1. Prepared on 11 November 2026. Effective date: to be set after approval.

This policy explains data processing in FamilyStars, an app for family tasks, achievements, rewards and challenges. Processing depends on the storage mode. The app is designed for parents and children aged 6 and over, with parents managing the family space, and also provides tasks for adults.

1. Storage modes

Local storage: Family data is stored in the app on the device. This mode does not require a Google account and does not upload family data to the app's cloud merely because it is used. Android system backups and sharing chosen by the user are separate, as explained below.

Local network synchronization (LAN): Family data and supported operations travel between paired devices on the local network. Synchronization uses local addresses, device identifiers, pairing and verification information, and records to coordinate operations and prevent duplicates. Paired devices may retain copies; disconnecting a device does not guarantee erasure of its saved copy.

Developer Cloud: Family data uses Firebase services, including Cloud Firestore for storage and Firebase Authentication for authentication. The developer manages this project. Administrative permissions may permit access to data to operate and protect the service and fulfil privacy requests.

Private Cloud: Users connect and manage their own Firebase project, including its permissions and deletion. The app developer has no default administrative permission to read or delete family content in that project. Google/Firebase still provides services, and administrator sign-in may use the authentication flow described below.

Some session information, settings and pending operations are also stored locally when using cloud modes to support operation and synchronization.

2. Data processed

Depending on the feature and mode, this may include:

  • Family name, names entered by parents for children and adults, and internal person identifiers.
  • Task text and schedules, achievements and their status, rewards, challenges, progress, archives and reports.
  • Administrator email, basic account information returned by Google, authentication user identifiers, family membership, roles, devices and invitations.
  • Technical device identifiers, device codes, optional names, and pairing and synchronization information. These differ from hardware phone identifiers such as IMEI.
  • Private Firebase project settings and connection information saved on the device when using that mode.
  • Derived verification values for some page PINs, local protection settings and activation of biometric authentication. Verification values may be synchronized as part of family data; a page lock does not isolate that person's data from other family members at database level.
  • A limited security record related to family deletion, described under retention.
  • Information you voluntarily send in support or privacy requests.

Authentication and hosting providers may process operational data such as IP addresses and browser or client information to operate and protect their services, even when the app does not store it in family documents.

We recommend a child's first name or nickname and only information necessary for the task. Free text may reveal personal information; avoid unnecessary sensitive information.

3. Purposes

Data supports tasks, achievements, rewards, reports, storage and synchronization in the chosen mode; authentication, memberships, devices and invitations; protection features; reminders and widgets; responding to requests and carrying out deletion; and detecting abuse and protecting the service.

Version 1.6.74 contains no advertising, and family data is not used for sale or targeted advertising. The examined flows contain no commercial analytics service or automatic crash-log upload through Analytics or Crashlytics. Local operational logs may exist, and system and service providers may process their own operational data under their policies.

4. Google sign-in and children's access

Local and LAN use does not require Google sign-in. Cloud administrator sign-in and management currently depend on Google; there is no alternative providing the same cloud administrator functions without Google.

Children do not need a Google account to use their interfaces. Cloud invitation joining may create a technical Firebase authentication identity without asking the child for an email, linking the device and membership. This identity can be associated with the family and is not a guarantee of anonymity.

Administrator sign-in uses basic account information available for authentication. The app does not request access to Gmail messages, Google contacts or Drive files, and does not receive the Google password. On Android, sign-in may open a helper page hosted on GitHub Pages using Google's sign-in service, then return the authentication result to the app.

5. Children and access within the family

Parents create children's data and manage their tasks and permissions. Children may add achievement information through available functions. A parent or guardian should handle family setup, cloud linking and any parental consent required by the processing and applicable law.

Administrators can view family data. Names, tasks and achievements may also be visible to other family members through app functions and settings; a family space is not a separate private account for each child. Parental management does not remove the developer's responsibilities for data processed by the service.

An authorized parent or guardian may contact us about their child's data. We verify their authority proportionately to protect the child and other family members.

6. Permissions and display on the device

  • Camera: Scanning invitation and pairing QR codes. Frames are analyzed locally in this flow, without recording audio or uploading or saving images as task evidence. Task photography and sound recording are not available in the covered version.
  • Notifications: Local reminders. Reminder text may appear on the lock screen depending on device settings.
  • Biometrics or device authentication: Checking access to protected areas when enabled. The app uses the system's authentication result; its app flow shows no storage or upload of fingerprint or face templates.
  • Network: Cloud services, local paired devices and connectivity status.
  • Device reboot events and keeping the processor awake: Appropriate background work such as restoring reminder schedules; these permissions do not themselves grant access to additional personal content.

Widgets may show the family name, people's names and progress on the home screen. An in-app PIN does not guarantee concealment of widget or notification content. Available app and system settings can control display.

7. Android backup and device transfers

The app permits Android to back up eligible data according to device settings, the backup service and applicable rules. Cloud backups may happen automatically during ordinary use. Data may be restored on reinstallation or device setup, or transferred directly between devices.

The usual Google system backup is separate from the Firebase project storing family data and is subject to the user's account and backup settings. We do not promise all data is backed up or fully restorable; some access or pairing information may require setup again.

This is separate from LAN synchronization and the in-app backup export/import feature, which is currently unavailable. Deleting data from the app's service does not replace managing device and account backups.

8. Recipients and sharing

Cloud modes use Google/Firebase for authentication and storage. GitHub Pages may host the administrator sign-in helper. These services receive the data necessary for their part of the flow and operational connection data under their settings and terms.

Within the family, data is available to authorized people and linked devices. When you copy or share a report, you choose the recipient; their copy is subject to their practices. Data may also be provided to authorized bodies where required by an applicable legal obligation.

Some processing may take place outside your country. Service processing and operational-copy locations vary; selecting a database location does not place all authentication and storage services in one country.

See Google Privacy, Firebase Privacy and Security, and GitHub Privacy.

9. Retention and security records

Family data needed for the service remains until deleted or its retention purpose ends. Archive settings affect how many recorded previous days are retained through cleanup during operation and synchronization. They are not a promise to erase all data after a fixed number of calendar days.

Support and privacy correspondence is used to process and follow up requests. Personal information is reviewed and deleted when no longer needed to follow up the request and protect related rights, unless a specific legal obligation requires retaining some of it. Correspondence is not used for marketing.

After a family deletion in Developer Cloud, a limited security record may be kept to detect abuse and protect the service. It may contain a family identifier, hashes derived from the deleting user's email and device identifier, dates, family-member counts, deletion outcome and failure stage. It contains neither task text nor children's names. Derived values are not fully anonymous.

The proposed commitment is to keep this record for no more than 270 days from creation, deleting it through manual review and cleanup within that period. Evidence of that operating procedure is required before this proposed policy is approved. We may delete it earlier when no longer needed. This does not authorize keeping the original family data or authentication account for that period.

Service deletion is handled below. Limited traces may remain within service providers' erasure and backup cycles. Firebase, for example, states that removing Authentication information from live and backup systems after user deletion is initiated may take up to 180 days. This differs from our request fulfilment period and the limited security record, and does not mean the account remains usable during that period.

10. Account and data deletion requests

A. Developer-managed cloud

Email familystars.support@gmail.com, preferably from the account's email, with:

  1. The email of the app account to delete.
  2. Family name and identifier if available, identifying the relevant family if the account is linked to several.
  3. A statement that the request concerns Developer Cloud.
  4. Either “I deleted the family data in the app and request completion of deletion of my account and its links” or “I request deletion of my account and associated data and have not deleted them in the app.” You may also request deletion of a specified family's data while keeping the account.

Prior in-app deletion is not required. If you say you deleted data, we check what remains associated with the account rather than assuming Authentication deletion alone is sufficient.

You do not need to be the founder to request deletion of your own account. Deleting a whole shared family space requires checking authority and other members' rights. We may request limited additional information to verify or distinguish accounts, or explain the shared-space implications.

We process the request and carry out deletion from the service we manage within 30 days of receipt, contacting you for verification or scope clarification without undue delay. Retention afterwards is limited to the exceptions described here and applicable legal requirements.

This deletes the app account, including Firebase Authentication, not your Google or Gmail account. We do not request Google passwords, verification codes, app PINs or Firebase project keys.

B. Deleting family data in the app

Authorized administrators can use “Family settings” → “Permanently delete all family data,” depending on mode and permissions. With multiple administrators, required approvals must be completed. This affects the target family space and its members, but does not automatically delete Firebase Authentication accounts. Removing local access information or disconnecting a device does not delete the cloud family space.

C. Private Cloud

The user or project owner handles in-app family deletion and manages Authentication records and residual data in their Firebase project. An authorized owner can use Firebase Console → Authentication → Users to delete only the accounts concerned after checking the effects. Deleting the whole project is not necessary.

The app developer cannot carry out administrative deletion in a private project without permissions, which are not required for ordinary guidance. We can explain the steps without asking for service keys or administrative access for a routine request.

D. Local data and other devices

Manage local data on devices that hold it, through app functions or Android app-storage settings. Deletion on one device does not guarantee deletion on paired or independent devices or system backups. Manage these copies through their devices and accounts. Uninstalling does not imply deletion of cloud data or the Authentication account.

11. Security and your rights

Cloud connections use service-provided transport protection, and LAN uses encryption and verification between devices. Authentication, memberships, roles and locks help limit unauthorized access. They are not an absolute security guarantee, nor do they mean cloud content is encrypted so that the project administrator cannot read it.

You or an authorized guardian can request access, correction or deletion, ask about processing, or exercise applicable legal rights. We verify requests as necessary to protect your data and others' data.

12. Changes and contact

This policy may change when data, recipients, purposes or features change. We update its date and provide additional notices or consent where required. The policy and disclosures will be updated before introducing image or audio task evidence, if added later.
